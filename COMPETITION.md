# Handwritten Journal — Competitive Landscape

Survey of what already ships on the App Store, and where this app sits relative to it.
Researched **2026-09-01** from App Store listings and third-party reviews; every claim
below has a link in §8. Prices and features are as listed on that date — App Store
listings change, so re-check before quoting any of this externally.

Companions: `DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md` (what the app does), `WIREFRAME_SPEC.md` (measurements),
`STYLE_GUIDE.md` (palette and type).

## 0. The short version

**Nothing on the store does the whole loop.** The market splits into three clusters, and
this app sits at the intersection of all three — an intersection no shipping product
currently occupies:

1. **Letter-tracing / handwriting instruction** — teaches formation, grades the ink,
   supplies the words. (Writing Wizard, iTrace, Kaligo, LetterSchool…)
2. **Kids' journals and diaries** — the child's own life is the content, handwriting is
   merely an input method and is never taught or graded. (DiaryZapp, iDiary for Kids,
   Me: A Kid's Diary…)
3. **Dictation / narrative capture** — the child speaks, the device listens. Speech
   exists to help the child *avoid* writing. (Speech Journal, Apple Dictation, the
   dysgraphia accommodation stack.)

Every tracing app in cluster 1 supplies the words — a curriculum list, sight words, or a
word list a grown-up typed in. This app is the only one where **the child's own speech is
the content source**, which changes what tracing is *for*: they are not practising "cat",
they are writing down what happened to them.

The two nearest single-feature matches are **Writing Wizard** (custom word lists, stroke
order, Apple Pencil, per-profile settings, replayable tracing history) and **Handwriting
Tracing Practice** (speak any text → it becomes traceable handwriting). Each is roughly
one feature away from the pitch. See §6.

## 1. Cluster 1 — Letter tracing and handwriting instruction

Closest to us on *mechanics*: guide glyph, pen, feedback.

### 1.1 Writing Wizard — the nearest competitor overall

[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/writing-wizard-learn-to-write/id1305242235) ·
[School Edition](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/writing-wizard-school-ed/id631446426) ·
[Developer site](https://lescapadou.com/wp/en/writing-wizard-app/) — Pierre Abel /
L'Escapadou. Free, IAP **$2.49–$9.99**, rated 4+.

Overlaps with us on almost every mechanic we have:

| Their feature | Our equivalent |
|---|---|
| Custom word lists, with per-word voice recording | The dictated transcript (§5.3) — but ours is generated by the child, not typed by an adult |
| **Enforces stroke order**, with configurable behaviour on deviation | The order discount (§8.1a) — ours docks 20% and teaches, theirs blocks |
| Apple Pencil support, with touch input disabled | Pen/scroll separation by touch count (§11.6) |
| 16 fonts, US school handwriting styles | Five curated faces as a per-profile setting (§7.2) |
| Multiple user profiles, adjustable difficulty | Per-profile font + glyph size settings |
| Detailed reports — **replay and export past tracings** | The journal archive — but ours is a keepsake for the child, theirs is telemetry for the adult |
| Printable worksheets | Whole-journal export (frame 43) |

**Where it is not us:** the words come from a grown-up, the output is a progress report,
and there is no page that accumulates into a book.

### 1.2 iTrace — the occupational-therapy pick

[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/itrace-handwriting-practice/id645416621) ·
[Site](https://itraceapp.com/) ·
[Common Sense Education review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/itrace-handwriting-for-kids).
Free, **$3.99** premium.

Zaner-Bloser, Handwriting Without Tears and D'Nealian styles; left- and right-handed
modes; an animated hand holding a pencil that shows where to trace. Notably it **erases
incorrect tracing back to the previous step** — a harsher cousin of our remediation modal
(§8.1b), and evidence that "you don't get to move on until it's right" is an accepted
pattern with this audience. 350 built-in words plus unlimited custom words. Widely
recommended by OTs for fine-motor difficulty.

### 1.3 Kaligo — closest to our grading engine

[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kaligo/id1345325412) ·
[HundrED profile](https://hundred.org/en/innovations/kaligo-digital-handwriting-notebook) ·
[MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/kaligo/1345325412) — Learn&Go (France), founded 2013.

Machine-learning analysis of **each stroke on shape, direction and order**, with real-time
corrective feedback and a cloud store of stroke data for later analysis. That is
essentially our v2.8 order discount, built as a school product for ages 3–8. Stylus
required. Sold to schools/teachers with progress tracking and personalised activity paths.

**Read this as validation of the grading rule, and as the ceiling for it** — if the
per-letter order judgement ever needs to get smarter than hand-fitted formations, this is
the prior art.

### 1.4 Handwriting Tracing Practice — the closest *single feature* match

[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/handwriting-tracing-practice/id1586385172) —
Vladimir Zavada. Free, IAP **$4.99–$8.99**, 4+, aimed at ages 6–8.

Three sections: Letters, Pairs (a matching game), and **Texts — type or *speak* any text
and it appears instantly in handwriting to trace**, with voice controls specifically for
children who cannot yet type. Cursive in D'Nealian, HWT, Zaner-Bloser, UK and SK/CZ
forms; print in Manuscript and SK/CZ. Exports as a cursive PDF to print or post.

**It has our input mechanism and none of our output.** No accuracy grading at all —
feedback is stars bursting from the brush tip and the letter name spoken aloud — and no
persistent journal. This is the one product that has already answered "can a child speak
their own tracing content?" with yes.

## 3. Cluster 3 — Dictation and narrative capture

**Speech Journal** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speech-journal/id436945985) ·
[Common Sense review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/speech-journal) ·
[Mobile Education Store](http://mobile-educationstore.com/apps/narrative-skills/speech-journal))
is the conceptual ancestor. The child picks or takes a photo and records themselves
talking about it. The classroom practice built around it is the tell: by third or fourth
grade the child **writes the story on paper while replaying their own recording**,
stopping and starting the iPad to keep up.

That is this app, performed manually, on paper, with no guide and no feedback. Speech
Journal is the strongest existing evidence that the core idea is a real teaching practice
and not an invention — and that nobody has closed the loop in software.

Below it sits the generic tier, where dictation exists so the child can *skip* writing:
Apple Dictation, Google Docs Voice Typing, Gboard, Dragon, and the dysgraphia
accommodation stack
([EBL](https://eblcoaching.com/best-apps-for-your-child-with-dysgraphia/) ·
[NY Family](https://www.newyorkfamily.com/dysgraphia-best-apps-child/) ·
[Braintrust](https://braintrusttutors.com/speech-to-text-tools-for-kids/)).

**Positioning note.** Our speech is the opposite of theirs. In the accommodation stack,
speech-to-text is the finished artefact; here it is scaffolding that the child's own hand
then overwrites and replaces.

## 5. What is genuinely unoccupied

Four things, and only the first is a single feature:

1. **Dictation as the content source for tracing.** Only Handwriting Tracing Practice
   does this, and it grades nothing.
2. **The guide becoming their ink in place** (§11.11) — finished lines stop being guide
   text and stay exactly where they were written. Every competitor moves the child to a
   results screen; the page above the hand *is* the record here.
3. **The archive as a keepsake book rather than a progress report.** Writing Wizard's
   replayable tracings are the closest analogue and they are framed as parent telemetry.
4. **Teach-on-failure gated to the specific letter that failed, in the middle of real
   writing** (§8.1b) — not in a drill, not as a separate practice mode, and not by
   blocking the pen (iTrace's rewind) but by owing one lesson for one letter.

## 6. Competitive risk

| Risk | Who | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| Dictation added to custom word lists | Writing Wizard | One feature. They already have voice recording per word, profiles, stroke order, and tracing history. |
| Scoring + saved entries added | Handwriting Tracing Practice | One or two features. They already have speak-to-trace and PDF export. |
| Consumer/journal framing on the school engine | Kaligo | Unlikely — B2B schools motion, but the grading is ahead of ours. |

Neither of the first two gets the journal *framing* for free — "your child's year, in their
own hand" is a product thesis, not a feature list. But both are close enough that the
framing is the moat, not the mechanics.

---

## 7. Pricing norms

The category is overwhelmingly **free to download with IAP in the $3–$10 band**:

- Writing Wizard — free, IAP $2.49–$9.99
- iTrace — free, $3.99 premium unlock
- Handwriting Tracing Practice — free, IAP $4.99–$8.99

School/classroom products (Kaligo, iTrace classroom, Writing Wizard School Ed.) are sold
separately on a per-seat/institution basis. Age rating across the tier is 4+.
