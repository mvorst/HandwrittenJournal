## 2. The idea, explained three ways

**For someone in a hurry.** Handwriting practice is dull and journalling is not. If the
sentence a child practises is one they wanted to say, practice stops being practice.

**For a parent.** Most tracing apps hand a child a word — cat, dog, ball. This one asks
what happened to them today and hands *that* back to be written. The page is worth writing
because it is theirs, and the book that piles up is a real reward, not a sticker.

**For a teacher or therapist.** Dictation is used as scaffolding, not as an accommodation.
The child's own spoken sentence becomes the guide text; they write over it with per-letter
accuracy feedback and stroke-order judgement; the guide is removed line by line, leaving
their handwriting in place; the finished page is archived with its transcript. It closes the
loop that Speech Journal-style classroom practice does by hand on paper.

## 3. Audience messages

### Parents

- **Lead:** They will write it because they said it.
- **Support:** Green ink inside the letter, red outside, while they write. A letter drawn
  backwards gets taught on the spot. Every finished page joins the journal, newest first.
- **Reassure:** Their journal never leaves the iPad. No account, ads, sign-in, analytics,
  crash reporting, or audio recorded. The basic app is always free. You choose the letter
  style and size; nothing has to be earned.
- **Close:** Do it for a day and you have a page. Do it for a year and you have a book in
  their own hand.

### Grandparents and gift-givers

- **Lead:** The gift is the book they write.
- **Support:** Export any page, or the whole journal, as a PDF and print it.

### Occupational therapists, tutors, specialists

- **Lead:** Formation feedback inside meaningful writing, not in a drill.
- **Support:** Per-letter accuracy scoring; stroke order and direction judged against
  ball-and-stick print formations; a wrong-order letter triggers a demonstrate-then-trace
  lesson for that letter, once per word; a practice sheet with every letter and numeral
  animating its strokes; Apple Pencil; left-handed layout; colour-blind ink pair; progress
  kept per font-and-size setting so a change of size never looks like regression.
- **Be honest:** Not a clinical tool. Print only, one letter style family (five typefaces,
  all print), English only. Grading is a difficulty dial set by a grown-up, not a norm.

### Teachers (K–2)

- **Lead:** A ten-minute writing centre that runs itself and leaves something for the
  portfolio.
- **Support:** Several children on one iPad, each with a profile and an optional PIN; a
  dated page per sitting; PDF export for the conference folder; the practice sheet as a
  warm-up; no logins, no Wi-Fi needed.
- **Be honest:** No teacher dashboard, no class roster, no sync between iPads, iPad only.

### Administrators and technology coordinators

- **Lead:** The children's work never leaves the iPad, and the app sends no data.
- **Support:** App Privacy is Data Not Collected; no account, advertising, analytics,
  crash reporting, or data sharing; on-device speech recognition; free through Apps and
  Books, nothing to license; a one-page data statement confirms this.

### Press and reviewers

- **Lead:** The only app where the child's own spoken words become the handwriting they
  practise — and where the page is kept, not scored and thrown away.
- **Support:** Independent developer; free, with no ads; offline by design; the
  competitive gap in one table (`COMPETITION.md` §4).

## 5. Objections and answers

| They say | We say |
|---|---|
| "Does it need an Apple Pencil?" | Handwritten Journal is designed for Apple Pencil — any model your iPad supports. |
| "My child can't be understood by Siri." | The words stay editable until they are written, so a misheard word never has to enter the record. And there is always *Type it instead*. |
| "Is it a subscription?" | No. The basic app is always free, with no ads and no account. Some additional features may be paid later; there is no subscription. |
| "Why is it free?" | Because we want children to thrive. Some additional features may be paid, because we will never sell your data. Nothing a child has written will ever sit behind a purchase. |
| "Where does the data go?" | The journal stays on the iPad; the only way it leaves is a PDF a grown-up exports. The app does not collect or transmit personal, device, analytics, or crash-reporting data. |
| "What exactly do you collect?" | Nothing. The app does not collect or transmit personal, device, analytics, or crash-reporting data. |
| "What if we lose the iPad?" | Export the book to Files or iCloud Drive now and then; the whole journal is one PDF. There is no cloud sync yet. |
| "Is it cursive?" | No. Print only, in five typefaces. |
| "Does it teach reading?" | No. It assumes the child can read the sentence they just said. |
| "Which handwriting programme does it follow?" | Ball-and-stick print, the order most US classrooms teach. It is not a licensed programme. |
| "Can two children share it?" | Yes. Each gets a profile with their own journal, font and size, and an optional PIN. |
| "Is there a teacher view?" | Not in this version. Each child's pages export as a PDF; a summary for teachers is on the list. |
| "Android? iPhone? Chromebook?" | iPad only. |

## 6. Voice

The app's own copy is written for a child and a grown-up in the same sentence, and the
marketing keeps that voice.

**Do**

- Sentence case. Short sentences. Plain words: *grown-up*, not *parent or guardian*.
- Say what the child does, then what happens: "They say it. It appears. They write it."
- Name the promise before the feature: the book, then the grading.
- Use *their* — their words, their page, their book.
- Be honest about what it is not; it reads as confidence.

**Don't**

- Exclamation marks. Ever.
- *Gamified, AI-powered, revolutionary, magical, unlock, level up, boost, master.*
- *Clinical, therapy, dysgraphia, intervention, evidence-based* — we are not those things and OTs will respect the line.
- Fake urgency, countdowns, "limited time".
- *The app is free forever*, *no in-app purchases ever*, *nothing to buy* — say *the basic app is always free*, with *basic*, and *some additional features may be paid* (`GO_TO_MARKET_PLAN.md` §4.1).
- Overbroad privacy claims that contradict the policy. The app's current, accurate claim is
  *Data Not Collected*: no personal, device, analytics, or crash-reporting data is sent.
- *A finger works*, *Pencil required* — the app is designed for Apple Pencil.
- Screenshots or clips with children's faces or names. Hands and pages only.
